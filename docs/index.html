What it does

Everything between a posting and your application.

Goes to the source, and cuts out the middlemen

Lookout reads companies' own applicant systems (Greenhouse, Lever, Ashby, Workable, SmartRecruiters, Recruitee, Oracle, Workday, YC) directly, alongside LinkedIn, Hacker News and remote-job boards. When the same role shows up in five places it becomes one card, and Apply opens the most direct link: short links are followed to where they land, and each role tells you before you click whether Apply opens the employer's own listing. Re-posts by recruiters are folded into the original, or clearly labelled when the original can't be found.

Job board→Recruiter's page→The company's own listing

Alerts on your phone

Save a set of filters as a view and turn on its bell. New matches arrive through the free ntfy app. Each view can go to a different phone, so one computer can scout for a group of friends.

Follows the money

Companies that just raised a round are about to hire. Lookout reads funding news and new YC batches, finds those companies' career pages, and checks them every couple of minutes.

Filters that mean it

Field, role, internship or full-time, years asked for, visa, remote, country, pay, degree, internship term, funding stage and skills. Click once to include, twice to exclude. Roles that want more experience than you have are hidden unless you ask to see them.

Who to write to

For each role Lookout looks for a published hiring email and the founders' public profiles, and drafts a short outreach note from your profile that you can edit and send yourself.

A pipeline for what you've applied to

Save, applied, contacted, interview, rejected. Notes on each role. It lives in the same place as the search, so nothing gets lost in a spreadsheet.

Finds new sources, or uses yours

Every link Lookout sees is checked for a careers board it doesn't know yet, and it searches GitHub for new community job lists. Prefer your own? Turn that off and start from a list a friend shared.

Filters

Say exactly what you're after. Once.

Every filter shows how many roles it would leave before you click it. Save a combination as a view, and the same rules decide which roles reach your phone.

  • Include or exclude anything. Click an option once to include it, again to exclude it, once more to clear it. Rule out citizens-only roles, Java, or a whole field.
  • Words, in the whole posting. Show only roles that mention “python” or “rust”; hide ones that mention “senior”. It reads the full description, not just the title.
  • My skills, in one click. Tick everything on your profile and see only roles that use it.
  • Experience that means it. Internship, new grad, years asked for. Roles that want more than you have stay out of the way.
  • Visa, remote, country, pay, degree, internship term, funding stage, and whether there's an email to write to.

Browser extension

Any job page, checked against you.

Found a role somewhere Lookout doesn't scan? Click the Lookout button and it reads the posting, compares it with your profile, and tells you whether Lookout already has it.

  • The skills gap, at a glance. Which of the role's skills you have and which you're missing, plus a warning when it wants more experience than you have or is citizens-only.
  • Already in Lookout? Where it was seen, when it was posted, and whether Apply there goes to the employer.
  • Save to pipeline. Roles from Workday careers sites or LinkedIn join the rest of your search.
  • Skills from your profiles. On your GitHub, Hugging Face, Stack Overflow, Kaggle or LeetCode profile the button shows a dot; click it to add the skills that page shows.
  • Only when you click. It reads the page you're on, sends it only to Lookout on your computer, and still compares skills while Lookout is closed.

Edge and Chrome. Included with the download; load it from the extension folder.

Matched to you

Drop in your résumé. It does the rest.

Lookout reads your résumé on your own computer and builds your profile from it, then ranks every role against what you can do.

  • Reads PDF, Word or pasted text. Skills, education, internships, jobs and projects with their tech stacks.
  • Technical skills come first. Languages, frameworks and tools drive the match. Words like “Operations” and “Communication” are kept, quietly.
  • Shows the gap. Each role tells you which of its skills you have and which you're missing.
  • Scans your links. Reads your GitHub repositories (languages, libraries, Docker and CI files) and your site, rates each skill strong, working or familiar, and suggests ones your work shows but your résumé doesn't list. Nothing is added without you.
  • Learns what you go for. Best match leans toward roles like the ones you save and apply to, and away from ones you hide.
  • Several profiles. One per person sharing the computer, or one per kind of role you're going for, each with its own views and pipeline.

Private by design

Your search is nobody else's business.

Lookout is not a website and has no servers. It is a program on your computer that reads public job listings.

  • No account. There is nothing to sign up for and nobody to give your details to.
  • Your data stays in one file on your disk. Résumé, profile, saved roles and notes.
  • No logins to other sites. It reads only what anyone can see without signing in, and never asks for your LinkedIn password.
  • Locked to your computer. Other websites can't reach it through your browser; the extension and your phone need a key you give them.
  • The code is there to read. Open source (AGPL-3.0), plain JavaScript, no third-party packages.
Questions

Good to know before you download.

Does my computer have to stay on?

Yes. Lookout scans only while it is running, so roles posted while your computer is asleep are found when it wakes up. A slower sweep of the last day catches those, but the alert will be later.

What do I need installed?

Windows 10 or 11 and Node.js 20 or newer. The Edge WebView2 runtime is also needed; Windows 11 has it already. There are no other packages to install.

Is it really free?

Yes, and open source under the AGPL-3.0 license. Phone alerts use ntfy, which is also free. Nothing in Lookout costs money or needs a paid key.

Is reading LinkedIn allowed?

Lookout reads LinkedIn's public job search, the pages anyone sees logged out, gently and without an account. Automated access is still against LinkedIn's terms of use, so that source is your call: clear the box in Settings and it is off.

What does the browser extension need?

Edge or Chrome. It comes in the download's extension folder: turn on Developer mode on the extensions page, choose Load unpacked, then paste the pairing key from Lookout's Settings. It reads a page only when you click its button.

Can I use it on my phone?

Alerts come to your phone through the free ntfy app. To browse Lookout itself on your phone, run it with HOST=0.0.0.0 and open the link from Settings → Other devices while on the same Wi-Fi; the link carries a key, so share it like a password.

Does it apply for me?

No. It finds roles, tells you fast, opens the right link and drafts an outreach note. You apply and send yourself.

Mac or Linux?

The scanner itself runs anywhere Node.js does: run npm start and open it in your browser. The packaged app with the tray icon is Windows only for now.
